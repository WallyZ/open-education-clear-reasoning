# Teaching Debate

## Authority And Purpose

Teaching Debate is a reusable Clear Reasoning course for learning how to
listen, clarify, construct, test, answer, and revise arguments. It supplements
the existing `clear-reasoning-foundations` course; it does not replace or
rename that program.

The supplied September 2026 Teaching Debate pack is starter input. Stable TD
lesson identifiers are retained where possible, but the pack is not itself the
authoritative public curriculum. Every lesson must be reconciled with current
Clear Reasoning objectives, drills, rubrics, sources, rights, and prerequisite
rules before promotion.

## Ownership Boundary

- Clear Reasoning owns transferable reasoning and debate methods, original
  public-safe exercises, general rubrics, and channel-neutral course text.
- `open-education-leadership` owns leadership-specific negotiation,
  coalition-building, institutional judgment, sales, fundraising, management,
  and crisis-leadership applications.
- Relevant subject repositories own historical, religious, scientific,
  medical, legal, and public-policy evidence dossiers.
- Private channel packages own series framing, episode briefs, adaptation
  choices, branding, and editorial decisions.
- `youtube-automation` owns authoring, evidence review, production, approval,
  and operator-gated distribution.
- `open-education-suite` owns learner state and educational runtime behavior;
  it remains content-agnostic.

Contested Questions is therefore a separate channel adaptation. It may use
Teaching Debate methods, but neither that channel nor its subject dossiers are
part of this course's authority.

## Pilot Sequence

The first accepted pilot is deliberately prerequisite-complete:

1. `TD001` - What Is Debate Actually For?
2. `TD002` - Listen Before You Answer

The broader review sample—TD001, TD002, TD004, TD013, TD037, and TD049—is not
a delivery sequence. TD004 depends on TD003; TD013 depends on TD001-TD012;
TD037 depends on TD001-TD036; and TD049 depends on TD001-TD048.

## Pilot Outcomes

### TD001 - What Is Debate Actually For?

- Outcome: distinguish truth-seeking, persuasion, competition, negotiation,
  and venting.
- Practice: state your objective, ask the other person's objective, and write
  one shared question.
- Assessment: identify the conversation type without equating winning with
  truth.
- Existing overlap: attention and distinction, dialectic and ordered
  questions, dialogue and disagreement.

### TD002 - Listen Before You Answer

- Prerequisite: TD001.
- Outcome: reconstruct another person's claim and concern before rebutting.
- Practice: listen, summarize, invite correction, and incorporate a specific
  correction.
- Assessment: the other speaker confirms the summary or identifies a concrete
  repair that the learner incorporates.
- Existing overlap: dialectic and ordered questions, dialogue and
  disagreement, and the debate-and-persuasion practice lab.

## Readiness Boundary

The pilot contains original course metadata and exercises only. It does not
include narration scripts, recordings, real-person quotations, clip analysis,
copyrighted source text, generated media, private learner data, production
state, or platform-policy decisions.

Source IDs from the starter pack remain provenance hints, not approved Clear
Reasoning sources. Source and rights status must remain `needs_reconciliation`
until the repository's source-packet and reviewer standards close those gates.

## Expansion Gate

Before another TD lesson becomes authoritative:

1. Resolve its full prerequisite closure.
2. Classify overlap as `reuse`, `extend`, `new`, `channel-only`, or
   `needs-review`.
3. Confirm whether the lesson is general reasoning or a Leadership-owned
   application.
4. Bind claims to approved sources and rights records where factual material is
   used.
5. Keep channel adaptation and publishing decisions outside this repository.
6. Run the canonical repository verifier and record human curriculum review.
